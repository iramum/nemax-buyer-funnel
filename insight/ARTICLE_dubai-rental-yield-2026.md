# Dubai rental yield in 2026: the number in the listing and the number in fact

*NEMAX Finance Insight, Market, November 2026, 7 min read*

You are looking at an apartment in Dubai to let. The listing says the area yields 7.1%. A market report says 6.3% for the same area and the same month.

Both numbers are real. They are counted from different data. If you plan on the higher one and the rent comes in lower, the purchase may not work the way you expected.

This guide shows rental yields by area for 2026 from two kinds of source. It explains why they differ and what to check before you commit.

The same report put the average gross yield for Dubai apartments at 6.6% in the third quarter of 2026.

Two things stand out. First, the gap is not the same everywhere. In Jumeirah Village Circle it is 0.8 points. In Arjan there is no gap at all. Second, the listing figure is not always the higher one. In Dubai South and Palm Jumeirah, registered deals showed more.

So the useful question is not "which area yields most?" It is "which number am I planning on, and where does it come from?"

## Why the two numbers differ

Yield is one year of rent divided by the price. Change either part and the yield changes.

A portal counts asking prices and asking rents from its own listings. Bayut says this in its own report: its yields use listing prices, not the prices buyers actually paid. A seller may ask more than the flat will sell for. A landlord may ask more rent than a tenant will sign for.

Registered data starts from deals that closed. The price is what the buyer paid. The rent is what the tenant agreed. It is closer to what happened, but it is older by the time it is published.

Other sources measure in other ways again. In Downtown Dubai, one source published 5.10% for 2026 and a dataset built from Dubai Land Department records showed 7.43%. On an AED 1.5 million apartment, a gap of more than 2 points is about AED 35,000 of rent a year.

[FIG 2: две цифры на одной квартире, AED 106,500 и AED 94,500]

On the same AED 1.5 million apartment in Jumeirah Village Circle, 7.1% means AED 106,500 of rent a year. 6.3% means AED 94,500. The difference is AED 12,000 a year, before any costs.

## Gross yield vs net yield: the costs the headline leaves out

Every yield in the table is gross. It does not include the costs of owning a let apartment. Buyers on Reddit ask about this more than anything else: what is a realistic yield after costs?

The main costs are these:

- **Service charges.** Each building has its own rate per square foot. Two towers in one community can differ a lot. In Jumeirah Village Circle, the 2026 rates for ten towers run from AED 14.98 to AED 20.42 per square foot a year.
- **Empty weeks.** A unit let for 11 months out of 12 has already lost more than 8% of its rent.
- **Letting and management fees**, if an agent finds the tenant or runs the unit.
- **Maintenance** between tenancies.

Here is a simple example: a typical one-bedroom apartment in Jumeirah Village Circle. In January to October 2026, the middle ready one-bedroom flat sold there was 812 square feet and cost AED 975,000. The service charge is the middle rate of the ten towers above, AED 17.74 per square foot for 2026.

| | AED |
|---|---|
| Price | 975,000 |
| Rent at 6.3% gross | 61,425 |
| Service charge (812 sq ft × AED 17.74) | minus 14,405 |
| One empty month | minus 5,119 |
| Rent left before fees and repairs | 41,901 |
| **Net yield** | **about 4.3%** |

Your numbers will be different. The point is the size of the step from gross to net. To find the real service charge, look up the building in the Dubai REST app or on the Dubai Land Department website. It shows the rate approved by RERA, the Dubai property regulator.

## Renewals, small samples and old figures

Three more things can move the number you see away from the number you get.

**Renewals.** Some figures mix new tenancies with renewals. In Dubai, the rental index limits how much rent can rise when a tenant renews. If the flat comes with a tenant, plan on the current rent until the contract ends.

**A few buildings.** A community yield is an average. Some rest on more than 200 buildings, others on fewer than 40. Where the sample is small, one new tower can move the whole figure.

**The date.** Prices and rents both moved in 2026. In the second quarter, prices and rents fell by about 2.5% compared with the first quarter. In the third, one consultancy recorded a small rise in prices, and another recorded a fall. About 136,100 new homes are scheduled for completion in 2027, and more supply can hold rents down. A yield from last year may not match today's prices.

## How to check a yield before you commit

Use this list before you plan income on any number.

1. Ask where the figure comes from: listings, registered deals or Dubai Land Department records.
2. Compare at least two sources for the same community and the same period.
3. Plan on the lower figure, not the one you saw first.
4. Look up the service charge for the exact building.
5. Check whether the rent comes from a new tenancy or a renewal.
6. Check the date of every figure.
7. Confirm when your money will be available, including the fees due on transfer day.

The last point is not about yield at all. It is the one that most often stops a purchase.

## The question after the yield: is your money ready on transfer day?

You have found the flat and agreed the price. Part of the money will come from somewhere else: a property you are selling, savings abroad, a business account.

On transfer day, the full amount is due at once. That is your share of the price plus the fees. The Dubai Land Department transfer fee is 4% of the price, and the agency fee is usually 2%. On an AED 1.5 million flat, that is about AED 90,000 on top of the price.

Since 1 February 2025, UAE banks have been told not to include the transfer fee and the agency fee in the mortgage. These must be paid from your own funds. If the money from the other sale or from abroad does not arrive in time, the purchase may be delayed or fall through.

## FAQ

**What is the average rental yield in Dubai in 2026?**
For apartments, the average gross yield was 6.6% in the third quarter of 2026, based on registered deals. It is a gross figure, so the net yield after costs is lower.

**Which area in Dubai has the highest rental yield?**
There is no single answer. Each source counts in its own way, so the same area can rank differently. In the September 2026 data above, Dubai South and Jumeirah Village Circle were near the top, depending on the source. Compare two sources and plan on the lower figure.

**How do I calculate rental yield in Dubai?**
Gross yield is one year of rent divided by the price, times 100. For net yield, subtract the service charge, empty weeks and letting fees from the rent first, then divide by the price.

**Is a 7% rental yield in Dubai realistic?**
As a gross listing figure, some communities show it. After costs, and based on registered deals, the same flat may give less. Check where the 7% comes from before you plan on it.

**Why do I need more cash than the deposit?**
Since 1 February 2025, UAE banks have been told not to include the transfer fee and the agency fee in the mortgage. You pay them on transfer day, together with your own share of the price.
