## 02 INTRO
You are looking at an apartment in Dubai as an investment. Before you commit, you want to know one thing: what will it earn?

A property portal shows a yield of 8.2%. A market report shows 6.7% for the same area. A third source gives a different number again.

If you plan on the higher number and the rent comes in lower, the purchase may not work the way you expected.

This guide explains seven reasons the published yield may not match what you will receive, what to check early, and what to do about it.

## 04 / 01 The yield is based on asking prices, not real sales
Buyers often ask which area gives the highest yield. The answer depends on who counts. Most property portals calculate yield from advertised prices and advertised rents. Bayut says this in its own report: it uses listing prices, not the prices buyers actually paid.

Registered data often shows a lower number. In Dubai Silicon Oasis, listings showed 8.23% in the first half of 2026. Registered data for August 2026 showed 6.69% for the same community.

WHAT TO CHECK
Ask the agent where the yield figure comes from: listings, registered data or Dubai Land Department records. If the source is not clear, do not use the number to plan income.

WHAT TO DO
Treat the listing yield as the highest likely figure, not a plan. Find the registered figure for the same community and plan on the lower number.

## 05 / 02 Different sources give different yields for the same area
Four well-known sources publish yields for Dubai. They measure at different points of a deal, so their numbers rarely match.

In Jumeirah Village Circle, one source published 6.36% and another 8.86% in 2026. On an AED 1.5 million apartment, a gap of 2.5 points is AED 37,500 of rent a year.

WHAT TO CHECK
Compare at least two sources for the community before you commit. Note the lowest and the highest figure, not only the one you saw first.

WHAT TO DO
Plan on a range, not one number. The real yield on your apartment will depend on the price you pay and the rent you agree.

## 06 / 03 The yield does not include costs
A common question is what the yield is after costs. Published yields are gross. They divide one year of rent by the price. They do not include service charges, empty weeks between tenants or letting fees.

Service charges are set for each building, so they can differ a lot inside one community. A unit that is let for 11 months out of 12 has already lost more than 8% of its rent.

WHAT TO CHECK
Look up the service charge for the specific building in the Dubai REST app or on the Dubai Land Department website. It shows the rate approved by RERA, the Dubai property regulator, for each project.

WHAT TO DO
Work out a net figure for this building: rent minus service charge, empty weeks and letting fees. Plan the purchase on that net figure.

## 07 / 04 The rent is from a renewal, not a new tenancy
Some yield figures mix new tenancies with renewals. In Dubai, the rental index limits how much the rent can rise when a tenant renews.

Yields based on renewals are often lower than yields based on new tenancies. A unit with a sitting tenant and an empty unit can give different results.

WHAT TO CHECK
Ask the agent whether the quoted rent comes from a new tenancy or a renewal. If the unit has a tenant, ask for the current tenancy contract and its end date.

WHAT TO DO
If the unit is let, plan on the current rent until the contract ends. If it is empty, check rents agreed recently for similar units in the same building.

## 08 / 05 The average rests on a few buildings
A community yield is an average. Some averages are based on more than 200 buildings, others on fewer than 40.

Where the number of buildings is small, one new or renovated tower can move the average. The figure then says little about the building you are looking at.

WHAT TO CHECK
Check how many buildings sit behind the community figure. The map on page 03 shows this in the n column.

WHAT TO DO
If the average rests on a few buildings, compare rents in the specific building instead. Recent tenancy contracts for similar units are a better guide.

## 09 / 06 Prices and rents are both changing
Yield is rent divided by price. In the second quarter of 2026, Dubai prices fell by 2.5% and rents also fell by 2.5%, compared with the first quarter. In the third quarter, average apartment prices fell by a further 4.8%. For the same homes, the fall was 1.7%.

About 162,500 new homes are expected to be completed in 2027. More supply can hold rents down. Savills also expects prime property values in Dubai to fall by around 10% in the second half of 2026.

WHAT TO CHECK
Check the date of every figure you see. A yield from last year may not reflect today's prices and rents.

WHAT TO DO
Past yields are not a promise. If you plan to sell in the next few years, look at the price forecast too, not only the rent.

## 10 / 07 Your cash is not ready on transfer day
You have agreed the price and plan to pay part of it from other funds. But that money is tied up in another property sale or in an account abroad.

Since 1 February 2025, UAE banks have been told not to include the transfer fee and the agency fee in the mortgage. These costs must be paid on the day of transfer. If the money is not ready, the purchase may be delayed or fall through.

WHAT TO CHECK
Confirm where your money is held and when it will be available. Ask the agent for the full amount due on transfer day, including fees.

## 11 WHAT BUYERS ASK
What buyers ask
Questions buyers asked in public Dubai property discussions on Reddit between August and October 2026, with plain answers.

**Which area in Dubai gives the highest rental yield?**
There is no single answer. Each source counts in its own way, so the same area can show different numbers. Use the table on page 03, compare the sources and plan on the lower figure.

**What is a realistic yield after costs?**
Published yields are gross. Take the rent and subtract the service charge for the building, a few empty weeks and letting fees. The result is lower, and it is different for every building. See page 06.

**A listing shows a high gross ROI. What should I ask first?**
Ask for the service charge of that building and where the rent figure comes from. A listing shows the seller's numbers, not what tenants actually paid. See pages 04 and 06.

**Can I raise the rent when my tenant renews?**
The rental index limits how much the rent can rise at renewal. If the unit already has a tenant, plan on the current rent until the contract ends. See page 07.

**Why do I need more cash than the deposit?**
Since 1 February 2025, UAE banks have been told not to include the transfer fee and the agency fee in the mortgage. You pay them in cash on the day of transfer, together with your own share of the price. See page 10.

**I already own a property. Can I use it to buy another one to let?**
Banks look mainly at your income. NEMAX looks at the property. If you need AED 1 million or more, capital can be raised against a property you already own in the UAE. See page 12.
