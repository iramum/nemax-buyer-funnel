# The Dubai Yield Map

## Why the yield your buyer sees may not be the yield they get

Your buyer is looking at an apartment as an investment. Before they commit, they ask a simple question: what will it earn?

You check a property portal and find a yield of 7.1%. A market report shows 6.3% for the same area. A third source gives a different number again.

If your buyer plans on the higher number and the rent comes in lower, they may lose trust in the purchase. Some buyers stop the deal at this point.

This guide explains seven reasons the published yield may not match what your buyer will receive, what to check early, and what to tell your buyer.

## The yield is based on asking prices, not real sales

Buyers often ask which area gives the highest yield. The answer depends on who counts. Most property portals calculate yield from advertised prices and advertised rents. Bayut says this in its own report: it uses listing prices, not the prices buyers actually paid.

Registered data may show a different number. In Jumeirah Village Circle, listings showed a yield of 7.10% in September 2026. Registered data for the same month showed 6.3%.

### WHAT TO CHECK

Ask where the yield figure comes from: listings, registered data or Dubai Land Department records. If the source is not clear, do not use the number to plan income.

### WHAT TO TELL YOUR BUYER

Explain that the listing yield is not a plan. Show them the registered figure for the same community and plan on the lower of the two.

## Different sources give different yields for the same area

Several well-known sources publish yields for Dubai. They measure at different points of a deal, so their numbers rarely match.

In Downtown Dubai, one source published 5.10% and another 7.43% in 2026. On an AED 1.5 million apartment, a gap of more than 2 points is about AED 35,000 of rent a year.

### WHAT TO CHECK

Compare at least two sources for the community before your buyer commits. Note the lowest and the highest figure, not only the one you saw first.

### WHAT TO TELL YOUR BUYER

Give them the range, not one number. Explain that the real yield on their apartment will depend on the price they pay and the rent they agree.

## The yield does not include costs

A common question is what the yield is after costs. Published yields are gross. They divide one year of rent by the price. They do not include service charges, empty weeks between tenants or letting fees.

Service charges are set for each building, so they can differ a lot inside one community. A unit that is let for 11 months out of 12 has already lost more than 8% of its rent.

### WHAT TO CHECK

Look up the service charge for the specific building in the Dubai REST app or on the Dubai Land Department website. It shows the rate approved by RERA, the Dubai property regulator, for each project.

### WHAT TO TELL YOUR BUYER

Help them work out a net figure for this building: rent minus service charge, empty weeks and letting fees. Plan the purchase on that net figure.

## The rent is from a renewal, not a new tenancy

Some yield figures mix new tenancies with renewals. In Dubai, the rental index limits how much the rent can rise when a tenant renews.

Yields based on renewals are often lower than yields based on new tenancies. A unit with a sitting tenant and an empty unit can give different results.

### WHAT TO CHECK

Ask whether the quoted rent comes from a new tenancy or a renewal. If the unit has a tenant, ask for the current tenancy contract and its end date.

### WHAT TO TELL YOUR BUYER

If the unit is let, plan on the current rent until the contract ends. If it is empty, check rents agreed recently for similar units in the same building.

## The average rests on a few buildings

A community yield is an average. Some averages are based on more than 200 buildings, others on fewer than 40.

Where the number of buildings is small, one new or renovated tower can move the average. The figure then says little about the building your buyer is looking at.

### WHAT TO CHECK

Check how many buildings sit behind the community figure. The map on page 03 shows this in the n column.

### WHAT TO TELL YOUR BUYER

If the average rests on a few buildings, compare rents in the specific building instead. Recent tenancy contracts for similar units are a better guide.

## Prices and rents are both changing

Yield is rent divided by price. In the second quarter of 2026, Dubai prices and rents both fell by about 2.5%, compared with the first quarter. In the third quarter, the picture was mixed. One consultancy recorded a small rise in prices, and rents returned to their March level. Another recorded a 4.8% fall in average apartment prices.

About 136,100 new homes are scheduled for completion in 2027. More supply can hold rents down. Savills also expects prime property values in Dubai to fall by around 10% in the second half of 2026.

### WHAT TO CHECK

Check the date of every figure your buyer sees. A yield from last year may not reflect today's prices and rents.

### WHAT TO TELL YOUR BUYER

Explain that past yields are not a promise. If they plan to sell in the next few years, they should look at the price forecast too, not only the rent.

## The buyer's cash is not ready on transfer day

Your buyer has agreed the price and plans to pay part of it from other funds. But that money is tied up in another property sale or in an account abroad.

Since 1 February 2025, UAE banks have been told not to include the transfer fee and the agency fee in the mortgage. These costs must be paid on the day of transfer. If the money is not ready, the purchase may be delayed or fall through.

### WHAT TO CHECK

Ask where the buyer's money is held and when it will be available. Confirm the full amount due on transfer day, including fees.

## What your buyers ask

Questions buyers asked in public Dubai property discussions on Reddit between August and October 2026, with plain answers.

**Which area in Dubai gives the highest rental yield?**

There is no single answer. Each source counts in its own way, so the same area can show different numbers. Use the table on page 03, compare the sources and plan on the lower figure.

**What is a realistic yield after costs?**

Published yields are gross. Take the rent and subtract the service charge for the building, a few empty weeks and letting fees. The result is lower, and it is different for every building. See page 06.

**A listing shows a high gross ROI. What should I ask first?**

Ask for the service charge of that building and where the rent figure comes from. A listing shows the seller's numbers, not what tenants actually paid. See pages 04 and 06.

**Can I raise the rent when my tenant renews?**

The rental index limits how much the rent can rise at renewal. If the unit already has a tenant, plan on the current rent until the contract ends. See page 07.

**Why do I need more cash than the deposit?**

Since 1 February 2025, UAE banks have been told not to include the transfer fee and the agency fee in the mortgage. You pay them in cash on the day of transfer, together with your own share of the price. See page 10.

**I already own a property. Can I use it to buy another one to let?**

Banks look mainly at your income. NEMAX looks at the property. If you need AED 1 million or more, capital can be raised against a property you already own in the UAE. It cannot be the property your golden visa depends on. See page 12.
